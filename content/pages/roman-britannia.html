Four essays tracing four hundred years of Rome at the edge of the world — from Caesar's first landings and Boudica's revolt to Hadrian's Wall, the rebel emperors of York, and the letter of 410 AD that left Britain to fend for itself — in English and Nepali.

  1. Part I: The Work of Giants

    How Britain stood at the misty edge of the classical world, why Julius Caesar crossed the Channel twice, and how Rome's shadow reached the island a century before conquest.

    Claudius's invasion of 43 AD with war elephants, Caratacus's guerrilla resistance, and Boudica's burning of Colchester, London, and St Albans.

    Agricola's northern campaigns, Hadrian's Wall, the Vindolanda tablets, and the structural trap that turned Britain's governors into rival emperors.
